The phone doesn't change

The layout follows the window's width, not the device. Below about 840 wide, each screen is the phone's, exactly as now; a tablet, a phone on its side, or a wide desktop window gets the two sides.

Everything else stays a column

The planner and Settings keep the phone's layout, centered, until they get wide layouts of their own. With index tabs, they sit beside the tabs like everything else.

Back and Escape

With two sides there's no back arrow on the open letter: the list is right there. Escape steps back on the right, once the desktop's keyboard work lands: a memory book goes back to her page, and a letter or page closes, leaving the list.

A new window's size

The first window is phone-sized today (440 × 860). To show the wide layouts from the start, a new window could open about 1180 × 780, smaller on a small screen. Windows you've already sized keep their size.

To choose

  1. The tabs: index tabs down the left, the bar under the list, or the bar across the bottom. My pick: index tabs, which give long letters the window's full height.
  2. The open letter or persona: a tab in your ink sticking out of its edge toward the open side, or its card raised off the page. My pick: the tab, which leaves the card itself as it is.
  3. Letters' width: a reading width (about 640, centered), or filling the side. My pick: a reading width, since lines get hard to follow past that.
  4. When Letters opens: the letter you last had open, or a quiet desk until you choose. My pick: the last one. A new unread letter stays unread until you open it.
  5. The gallery: beside the page you last opened, or the whole window until you open one. My pick: beside the page, so nothing jumps when you open one.
  6. A new window's size: about 1180 × 780, or the phone's 440 × 860 as now. My pick: 1180 × 780.
